# IntimaGuard Clinical Advisory Council

**Status**: Forming — Phase 1 (2026)
**Target size**: 5–8 founding advisors
**Time commitment**: ~4 hours per quarter

---

## Why the Council Exists

IntimaGuard's evaluation framework is only as credible as its clinical grounding. The six dimensions, rubric anchors, and harm pattern definitions are derived from peer-reviewed literature in attachment theory, mentalization research, parasocial relationship research, and clinical practice. But literature is not the same as clinical judgment.

The Clinical Advisory Council exists to close that gap. Advisors do not govern IntimaGuard — governance sits with the founder. They provide domain authority: the kind that comes from working with real people in intimate and relational contexts, and from understanding what harm actually looks like in practice rather than in theory.

The specific questions advisors are needed to answer:

- Are the six dimensions the right set? Are there clinically important harm patterns missing?
- Do the rubric anchors reflect clinical judgment? What would a score-2 response look like in your practice?
- Is the proposed weighting defensible? Should Scope Awareness carry as much weight as Transparency?
- Should "Identity & Relational Self-Perception" be added as a scored dimension, held as a qualitative flag, or deferred?
- Is the Emotional Response vocabulary (Supported / Comfortable / Uneasy / Unsettled / Concerned) clinically appropriate for human evaluators?
- Are the rubric anchors calibrated to detect subclinical harm — the patterns that read as benign in isolation but accumulate into damage over time?

## Domain Slots

The council is structured around domain slots, not individual names. Each slot fills a specific gap in the framework's clinical grounding. A founding advisor fills one primary slot; some advisors may bridge two.

| Slot | Why it's needed |
|---|---|
| **Attachment & parasocial relationship research** | Rubric calibration for Autonomy & Independence; grounding for the parasocial attachment induction construct; validation of the subclinical harm framing |
| **Sex therapy / intimacy coaching** | Scope and Boundary dimensions; what healthy intimate AI interaction looks like at score 5; clinical experience with relational harm patterns |
| **Crisis intervention / suicide prevention** | Crisis Awareness & Referral dimension calibration; crisis scenario design; validation of referral language and thresholds |
| **Human-computer interaction / digital wellness** | Platform evaluation methodology; canonical scenario design; behavioral signal validity; evaluation UX for non-expert evaluators |
| **Public health or health policy** | Regulatory translation; SB 243 / DSA / SAFE BOTs Act compliance framing; research grant positioning |

## What Advisors Do

**Quarterly:**
- Attend a 2-hour Advisory Council session (remote) to review framework updates, rubric calibration questions, and open research questions
- Provide written feedback on specific rubric anchors or dimension definitions when requested

**On joining:**
- Complete a structured rubric review — the eight Advisory Council questions in `METRICS_REFINEMENT.md`
- Validate or challenge the clinical basis of the dimension definitions most relevant to your domain

**Optionally:**
- Co-author a validation paper or white paper
- Submit scenarios from clinical experience (anonymized) to the scenario library
- Identify other potential advisors from your network

**What advisors do not do:**
- Govern IntimaGuard (no board seats, no voting rights)
- Review individual AI system evaluations
- Provide legal or regulatory opinions

## Recognition

Founding advisors receive:

- Named credit on the IntimaGuard website, GitHub repository, and published papers
- "IntimaGuard Clinical Advisory Council" affiliation for use in their own professional materials
- Early access to the SDK, leaderboard data, and research findings before public release
- Co-authorship on validation papers where their contribution warrants it
- Potential continuing professional development (CPD) hours — in discussion with relevant professional bodies

There is no financial compensation in Phase 1. If grant funding is secured in Phase 2, advisor compensation is a priority use of those funds.

## Who We Are Looking For

Clinical expertise and domain knowledge matter more than name recognition. We are specifically looking for people who:

- Have direct clinical or research experience with the harm patterns IntimaGuard is designed to detect — attachment disruption, parasocial dependency, relational capacity erosion
- Are willing to engage critically with the framework — to push back on rubric anchors that don't reflect clinical reality, not simply to validate what's already there
- Have interest in AI's psychological impact that goes beyond general concern — advisors who have encountered AI-adjacent harm patterns in their own practice or research are especially valuable
- Can commit to the time requirement reliably — four hours per quarter is the minimum; advisors who cannot sustain that are better positioned as contributors than as council members

We are actively seeking advisors who reflect the diversity of the populations most affected by companion AI harm. The evaluation framework should be calibrated by people who understand what harm looks like across different demographics, relationship structures, and cultural contexts.
